## Commentary: the bits worth arguing about

### “Life” that starts with a funeral
Chayei Sara and Vayechi are both named for living and mostly about dying. Classic Torah irony — and a secular-friendly reading: a life is measured by what keeps going after you. A grave paid for honestly. A son married. Two estranged brothers standing together at a grave. The parsha called *Life* is about legacy, not biography.

### Paying full price on purpose
Avraham refuses the theatrical “gift” and pays 400 shekels in merchant-grade silver in front of witnesses (23:16). Ramban (23:9) reads him as asking to buy *as if* it were a gift, at full value — and notes that Avraham only wanted the cave; Ephron pushed the whole field onto him “out of courtesy or trickery.” Why insist on paying? A gift can be clawed back; a witnessed public sale can’t. First Jewish land in Canaan is a cemetery plot, legally clean. Rashi (23:16) / Bava Metzia 87a: Ephron’s name loses a *vav* here because he “said much and didn’t do even a little.”

### How do you pick a person?
The servant’s test isn’t looks or dowry — it’s *chesed*, kindness beyond the ask (24:14). Water for a stranger is manners; volunteering ten camels is character. Ta’anit 4a still worries the method was risky (“she might have been lame or blind!”) — and God answered properly anyway. Good debate for the table: is character-under-pressure a fair hiring test? (Fiddler’s “Matchmaker, Matchmaker” and Netflix’s *Jewish Matchmaking* are light modern cousins — with a twist: here the girl gets asked, and she says yes.)

### Consent — אֵלֵךְ
“We’ll call the girl and ask her” (24:57). Rashi via Bereshit Rabbah 60:12: from here, a woman isn’t married off without her consent. Her answer is one word — *elech* — a callback to Avraham’s *lech lecha*, now said by Rivka, by choice. Same root. New speaker.

### Grief and comfort
Yitzchak is comforted “after his mother” only through love (24:67). Ramban: the Torah mentions a husband’s love *because* that is what finally comforted him. The midrash’s returning candle, dough-blessing, cloud, and open doors (Bereshit Rabbah 60:16 / Rashi) are a picture of a house coming back to life. Sarah’s tent is lit again — not replaced, continued.

### Telling the story twice
The servant’s almost-full retelling (24:34–49) is not padding. Bereshit Rabbah 60:8 via Rashi 24:42: the small talk of the patriarchs’ servants is dearer before God than the Torah of their children. Elsewhere laws get packed into single letters; here the text slows down for a kind act at a well.

### Brothers at the graveside
Yitzchak and Yishmael bury Avraham together (25:9). Talmud (Bava Batra 16b) / Rashi: Yishmael letting Yitzchak go first = repentance and reconciliation — part of the “good old age” promised to Avraham. The real ending of the matchmaking episode isn’t only the wedding. It’s two half-brothers at their father’s grave.

### Verified facts (from the research pack)
- **Ages:** Sarah 127; Avraham 175; Yishmael 137 — Genesis 23:1, 25:7, 25:17.
- **400 vs later Bible prices:** Jeremiah’s field 17 shekels (Jer 32:9); David’s threshing floor 50 (II Sam 24:24).
- **Machpelah = “double”** — Eruvin 53a: two chambers, or doubled with couples (Adam & Eve, Avraham & Sarah, Yitzchak & Rivka, Jacob & Leah — Rashi 23:2).
- **The servant is unnamed in Gen 24** — “Eliezer” is traditional (from 15:2). Fine to use; say so.
- **Rivka’s age is not in the Torah** — the famous “she was 3” is a later rabbinic calculation. Skip it for the story.
- **Sarah / Akedah shock** — midrash (Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer 32 via Rashi 23:2), not the plain text.
- **Keturah = Hagar?** — midrash (Rashi 25:1 via Bereshit Rabbah 61); Torah just names Keturah.
- **Camel archaeology** — Tel Aviv University (Sapir-Hen & Ben-Yosef, 2014) dated domesticated camels in the Aravah to the late 10th century BCE (NYT: “Camels Had No Business in Genesis”). Others point to earlier evidence elsewhere; treat as a debate, not a verdict.
- **Israeli playground game “חיי שרה”** — confirmed on Hebrew Wikipedia; dodgeball cousin / last-one-standing.
- **Haftarah echo** — I Kings 1:1 opens with the same “old, advanced in days” as Genesis 24:1.

### Pop culture on the sideboard
*Fiddler on the Roof* — “Matchmaker, Matchmaker”: the servant is the Torah’s first *shadchan*, but with a camel instead of Yente. Twist: in Fiddler the daughters rebel; here Rivka is asked and answers *elech*. Netflix’s *Jewish Matchmaking* (2023) is a light modern cousin. Ephron’s “what’s 400 shekels between friends?” has pure *Selling Sunset* energy — generous words, firm price, payment in front of the whole town gate.

### Questions for the family
1. The servant’s test was: do they help more than they were asked? What would *your* camel test be — for a friend, a partner, or a new teacher?
2. Why pay full price when someone offers a “gift”? When does honesty matter more than a bargain?
3. Two half-brothers at one grave — what does reconciliation look like in your family?
4. Grandparents: how did you two meet — was there a matchmaker, a well, or a camel involved?
5. Bonus for Noam & Alon: after lunch, teach everyone the rules of the playground game *Chayei Sara*.
